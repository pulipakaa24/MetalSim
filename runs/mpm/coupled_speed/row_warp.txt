`warp.sparse.bsr_mv` on Metal makes CUDA's choice between its tiled and one-thread-per-row kernels (`warp.config.metal_bsr_mv_tiles`, `WP_METAL_BSR_MV_TILES`, default on). Rows with many blocks (`nnz * block_size > 2 * tile_size * rows`, CUDA's heuristic) are summed by the tiled kernel: 64 strided lane sums, then `tile_sum`, whose Metal SIMD-group reduction is the CUDA shuffle tree. So both GPUs use the same summation order. Before, Metal summed every row serially in one thread. In Newton's implicit MPM, the rigidity operator's body rows (IJtm: one block per collider node of the body, about 1,170 in `example_mpm_twoway_coupling`) were latency-bound, and their cost grew with the contact area. Measured on the M4 Max: the IJtm product went from 29-40 to 12.1-12.9 ms per 50 calls (eager, synchronized). Captured two-way went from 54.8 to 40.4 ms/frame (L4: 31.0). Results change rounding only where a product takes the tiled path (two-way; Franka-Pour's rigidity products). MetalSim's pure-MPM floor scenes and ANYmal on sand are bitwise unchanged. Two-way repeats bitwise and stays inside the L4's perturbation ensemble. Off, results are bitwise those of before (Franka-Pour 30 steps). Probe: MetalSim `metalsim/physics/mpm/probes/bsr_mv_tiles_check.py`.
