2. Newton PR #4291 (stress-transpose normalization, Delassus majorization, plastic-strain reconstruction). It is a

## 2. Why the repose angle is low: evidence and hypotheses

**New evidence from the existing runs** (on `origin/main`, never commented on). The three heap estimators disagree by
more than the protocol's own 2° limit:

| voxel (R/dx) | fit, 25–75 % of height | cone volume, atan(3V/πr_toe³) | height/flank |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 (5) | 25.4 | 30.2 | 25.2 |
| 0.005 (10) | 26.3 | 31.5 | 26.4 |
| 0.0033 (15) | 27.2 | 32.8 | 28.0 |
| 0.0025 (20) | 27.8 | **33.3** | 27.8 |

At R/dx 20: h_max 38 mm, toe 82 mm, apex plateau 10 mm. The material volume (3.9e-4 m³) is ~30 % more than a cone
with the fitted slope holds (~3.0e-4 m³), and more than a frustum with that plateau (3.0e-4 m³). So the heap is
**convex**: a rounded crest with a steeper lower flank. The fit window, 25–75 % of a heap only 15 cells tall, sits
largely in the rounded part. The cone-volume angle is within 1° of atan μ = 34.2° and rises in step with the fit.
*(Inference: the bulk of the heap may already stand near atan μ, and the deficit may be a near-surface skin a few
cells thick.)*

Things that are **not** the cause, from the measurements and the code:
* Numerical dissipation. More damping stops flow earlier and steepens heaps, so it works the other way. The lift-speed
  plateau and 4× iterations (+0.3°) rule out under-convergence.
* Elasticity: E = 1e15 makes the material rigid.
* `air_drag`: it is added to a density-scaled node mass (~1,500), and drag × dt ≈ 0.008, i.e. ~5e-6 relative
  (`implicit_mpm_solver_kernels.py` `free_velocity`, `integrate_mass`).
* The unilateral offset: inactive with `critical_fraction = 0`.
* Floor friction: 1.0, above μ.

Ranked hypotheses (cheapest test first):

* **R1. Measurement and size effect (convex heap).** The fitted flank is the rounded crest. The slope deep in the heap
  approaches atan μ.
  *Test:* save h(r) per sector and fit the slope as a function of local depth in cells. Rerun with a heap twice as
  tall in cells (fill 10 cm at voxel 0.005). Pure analysis, ~2 min of GPU.
* **R2. Weak free-surface skin from the discretization.** P0 strain (one stress per cell) with Q1 velocity, at
  partially filled surface cells: stress and pressure are cell averages, so a 1–2 cell skin can shear at low
  confinement. This fits the ~+1° per refinement trend and the convex crest.
  *Test (config only):* `velocity_basis="B2"`, `strain_basis="Q1d"`/`"P1d"`, `integration_scheme="gimp"`, and
  27 → 64 particles per cell, at R/dx 10. Newton's draft [#4660](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/pull/4660)
  (2026-10-13, gdaviet) fixes P1d/Q1d strain modes that "can lose valid strain modes when their first moments cancel"
  in partially filled cells. If P1d/Q1d help but look noisy, that PR is the reason.
* **R3. Drucker–Prager stress-state geometry.** The yield cone is circular in the deviatoric plane: strength depends on
  the intermediate stress (Lode angle), unlike Mohr–Coulomb (see the DP/MC comparisons
  [Springer 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00603-012-0278-2) and
  [Reuleaux plasticity](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1158468/reuleaux-plasticity-improving-mohr-coulomb-and-drucker-prager)).
  A radially spreading, axisymmetric heap has hoop extension. With a coaxial flow rule this puts σ_θθ below p, raises
  J2, and makes the flank yield before τ/σ_n reaches μ. *(Inference.)*
  Scale of the effect: the MC angle that matches this cone in triaxial compression, sin φ = 3μ/(2√3 + μ), gives
  18.1 / 29.5 / 38.2° for μ = 0.4 / 0.68 / 0.9. Linear extrapolation of the fit to zero voxel lands near ~29–30° at
  μ = 0.68.
  *Test:* G1. A plane-shear layer should stop at atan μ if the rheology is right. If G1 passes and the cone stays low,
  the model is the cause. Also export `mpm:particle_stress` from the heap and plot sqrt(J2)/(μp) and the Lode angle
  along the flank.
* **R4. Solver defects fixed upstream after 1.5.2** ([#4277](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/issues/4277) /
  [#4291](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/pull/4291): a factor ½ in `_symmetric_part_transposed_op`, still
  present in our vendored copy and in the 1.7-dev install; Delassus diagonal majorization that "accounts for
  spherical/deviatoric coupling that the solver had been dropping"; plastic-strain reconstruction). For a rigid,
  cohesionless cone the converged solution is invariant to the stress scale (the cone is scale-free; yield_pressure is
  1e12, never reached), and 4× iterations changed nothing. So the expected effect is ≤ 0.5°. It is still worth
  backporting in the physically-right mode, because it changes convergence and anything with cohesion or elasticity.
  *Test:* a backport on a branch, at R/dx 10.

**What fixes it, depending on the outcome:**
* R1 / R2 → configuration: basis, resolution, ppc, plus a properly built repose measurement.
* R3 → either (a) calibrate μ per target material against G2 (config only; the incline then stands steeper than the
  heap, by the same few degrees), or (b) a code change: a Lode-angle-dependent yield surface (Mohr–Coulomb-like or
  Matsuoka–Nakai) in the rheology's local projection. (b) is no longer a second-order cone, so the DB16 local solver
  needs a new projection. It is substantial and only justified if (a) breaks G1 or G4.

## 3. Coupling fidelity

**C1. Excess drop penetration is the one-step exchange lag.** Our own data: lagged mode, 50 iterations,
v_impact = 1.716 m/s. Peak penetration 38.8 mm at dt 1/120 and 31.7 mm at dt 1/240: a difference of 7.1 mm, against
v·Δdt = 1.716 × (1/120 − 1/240) = 7.15 mm. In lagged mode the sand's decelerating impulse reaches MuJoCo one step late,
so the body travels about one extra step at speed. Extrapolated to dt → 0: ~25 mm at 50 iterations, ~16 mm for the
converged run (29.8 mm at 1/120). Both are inside the 10–25 mm band. The caveat is that this rests on two points, at
different solve settings.
*Fixes:*
* (a) A smaller coupled dt: config only, cost ∝ 1/dt.
* (b) Newton's proxy relaxation iterations (`SolverCoupledProxy.Config.iterations`, `proxy_relaxation_mode="aitken"`).
  They are in 1.5.2 and 1.7-dev (`.venv/.../coupled/solver_coupled_proxy.py:293, 1324`) and re-run both solvers over
  the same interval until the exchanged force is consistent. This is a code change in `MPMCoupling`; the cost is
  ≈ iterations × the coupled step.
* (c) Long term: a monolithic solve. Newton's `SolverCoupledADMM` is documented only for MJWarp + VBD; MPM support is
  unknown.

**C2. Half-voxel resting settlement is grid contact resolution.** It is "sub-voxel gaps" per gdaviet on #3473.
Collider nodes are active within ~0.25 voxel (S2), with velocity-level contact and no positional correction.
Expected ∝ voxel. The voxel-0.01 rest run (19.7 mm) used 50 iterations, so it is not evidence against this. Converged
reruns at voxel 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.005 are needed.
*Options:* `collider_basis="pic27"` (recommended in #1251), `project_outside_colliders` (Isaac's particle-level
push-out), and finer voxels near contacts.

**C3. Creep follows solve convergence** (measured). The physically-right mode runs a converged solve: tolerance 1e-6,
max_iterations ≥ 250, residual logged.

**C4.** Once C1 is removed, the remaining drop depth should follow Uehara with μ = tan(repose measured in G2). If the
material is effectively weak (R3), the depth will be too deep by the ratio of the tangents: tan 34°/tan 27.8° ≈ 1.3.

Other coupling literature, for the design of (b)/(c):
* [Hu et al. 2018 MLS-MPM/CPIC](https://yuanming.taichi.graphics/publication/2018-mlsmpm/): impulse-based two-way
  coupling, explicit.
* [Gast/Schroeder penalty coupling 2017](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~craigs/papers/2017-rigid-mpm/paper.pdf): tight
  coupling; shows a cube coming to rest in sand.
* Daviet & Bertails-Descoubes 2016 ([HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01310189)): rigid coupling inside the
  nonsmooth solve.
* [Yu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05046v1): convex, weakly coupled, asynchronous.
* [Daviet 2026 Mixed MPM](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/prl/mixed_mpm/): Newton's current solver family, with
  two-way rigid coupling demos. I could not read the PDF here (over the fetch size limit); its project page reports no
  repose or collapse validation.

## 5. Recommendation

### Benchmarks and pass criteria (physically-right mode)

| Bench | Pass |
|---|---|
| B1 tilting box (G1) | θ_fail within ±1° of atan μ for μ = 0.4 / 0.68 / 0.9 at ≥ 10 cells depth; ±2° at 5 cells |
| B2 repose (G2) | fit, cone-volume and height/flank estimators agree within 2°; angle within 2° of the target material's repose (dry sand 30–35°, chosen per preset); heap angle in [θ_stop(B1), θ_stop + 3°]; monotone and converging with R/dx, with the last refinement < 0.5° |
| B3 column collapse (G3) | 2D runout exponents 1.0 ± 0.1 (a ≤ 2) and 0.67 ± 0.07 (a ≥ 3); prefactors within 15 % of Lajeunesse 2005 / Lube 2005 for the calibrated μ |
| B4 sphere drop (G4) | d within 15 % of Uehara's law at h = 0.1–0.8 m, using μ = tan(B2 angle); fitted exponent of H 0.33 ± 0.05; d varies < 10 % between dt 1/240 and 1/480 |
| B5 footing (G5) | rest settlement ≤ 0.25 voxel and ≤ 3 mm, creep < 0.1 mm/s; bearing failure load within a factor of 2 of Vesic for φ = B2 angle; failure for μ ≤ 0.3 |

### Hypotheses, cheapest first

1. R1: measurement and size effect. Analysis plus one taller heap.
2. C1: exchange lag. A dt sweep (config); then proxy iterations (small code change).
3. R2: discretization skin. Basis, ppc, gimp (config).
4. R3: Drucker–Prager geometry. B1 vs B2, plus a stress export (config plus a probe).
5. C2: contact resolution. Voxel sweep converged, pic27, project-outside (config).
6. R4: backport #4291 (code, flag-gated).
7. Only if needed: a Lode-dependent yield surface (code, large), or a monolithic coupling.

### Physically-right mode vs parity mode

Parity mode stays bitwise Isaac:
* vendored 1.5.2 copy;
* staggered exchange for Franka-Pour;
* Isaac's solver settings;
* the parity tests unchanged.

The physically-right mode is a named `MPMConfig` preset (e.g. `fidelity="physical"`):
* **Config only:**
  * lagged exchange;
  * converged solve (tol 1e-6, iterations ≥ 250, residual logged);
  * coupled dt ≤ 1/480 near impacts, or MPM substeps with per-substep exchange;
  * voxel ≤ 0.01 under contacts;
  * whatever basis or ppc R2 selects;
  * calibrated μ per material preset.
* **Code, flag-gated:**
  * proxy relaxation iterations / Aitken in `MPMCoupling` (port from `SolverCoupledProxy`);
  * the #4291 backport as a kernel variant (the generated vendored copy stays byte-identical; the variant lives beside
    it and is selected by the flag);
  * new probes (tilting box, sphere drop, profile and stress export).
* **Only if R3 is confirmed and calibration is not enough:** a Lode-dependent yield surface.

## 6. First GPU batch (description only; no script yet)

"sand_fidelity_01". Everything except item 5 uses existing code paths or new probe files; there are no solver changes.
Total ≈ 45–60 min on the 16-inch.

1. **Repose diagnostics (R1, R3).** μ 0.68 at R/dx 10 and 20, and a fill twice as tall at R/dx 10. Save per-sector
   h(r) profiles and, at rest, particle positions plus `mpm:particle_stress`. Then compute the local slope vs depth in
   cells, and sqrt(J2)/(μp) and the Lode angle on the flank.
2. **Tilting box (R3, B1).** μ 0.4 / 0.68 / 0.9 at voxel 0.005, plus μ 0.68 at voxel 0.01. Record θ_fail (first
   sustained surface speed > 1 mm/s) and the post-avalanche angle.
3. **Discretization variants (R2).** Repose at R/dx 10, μ 0.68, varying one setting each from the baseline:
   `velocity_basis` B2, `strain_basis` Q1d, P1d, `integration_scheme` gimp, and 64 particles per cell.
4. **Drop dt sweep (C1).** Box drop from 0.15 m, lagged mode, converged solve (250 iterations, tolerance 1e-6), voxel
   0.02, at dt 1/120, 1/240, 1/480, 1/960. Repeat dt 1/480 at voxel 0.01. Check penetration against d₀ + v·dt.
5. **Rest voxel sweep (C2).** Converged rest test at voxel 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.005, with S2 and pic27, each with and
   without a `project_outside` call after each MPM step.
6. **Sphere drop (B4) smoke test.** D = 0.05 m at h = 0.2 m and dt 1/480, so the fixture is ready for the full
   Uehara sweep in batch 02.

Batch 02 depends on the outcome of batch 01:
* proxy iterations in `MPMCoupling` (C1 fix b);
* the #4291 kernel-variant backport;
* the full Uehara sweep;
* a column-collapse re-check with the calibrated μ;
* then the `fidelity="physical"` preset with its tests. The parity tests must stay bitwise.

### 8.1 The solve never reaches its tolerance: "converged" means capped at 250 iterations

logged: `MPMCoupling` installs `newton_compat` with `log=False` and captures the step.)*

**Solved elsewhere?** No report. Isaac Lab's default (250 iterations, 1e-4) would sit at the cap too.

**CUDA already shows it.** The L4 parity records (`runs/parity/isaaclab3_mpm*/**/solves.npz`, recorded with

### 8.5 Voxel 0.005 with S2: a Warp-fork allocator limit, not inherent to S2

The crash happens in S2 collider rasterisation (`_rasterize_colliders` → `fem.interpolate` → `bsr_set_from_triplets` →
`radix_sort_pairs`). The Metal fork's sort allocates its scratch with `scratch_ptr(nbytes)` as a **uint8** array of
`max(nbytes, 2 × previous)` elements. The failed request was exactly 3 GiB (3,145,729,280 bytes): double a previous
1.5 GiB buffer. Two defects:
1. the element count is in bytes, so it hits the int32 element limit at 2 GiB;
2. doubling overshoots what the sort needs.

pic27 at the same 1.57M particles ran, because it builds no S2 interpolation matrix.

It is the same *class* as the deterministic-plan crash, a Warp array limit, but **not the same allocator**:
* That crash ("3.5 G elements", MuJoCo Warp's fused update in deterministic mode) comes from `deterministic.py`'s
  scatter record buffers, sized as launch dim × records per thread and checked against
  `DETERMINISTIC_SCATTER_MAX_CAPACITY`. That is a record count above int32, not the byte-sized sort scratch.
* So P2 does not cover it. The deterministic path's own `uint8` sort-reduce workspace
  (`wp_deterministic_sort_reduce_workspace_size`) is a separate allocation and could hit the byte form of this bug too
  at larger sizes; worth checking when that work resumes.

**Fix (prerequisite P2, Warp fork):** allocate the scratch in 8-byte elements, and grow to at most
max(nbytes, min(2 × previous, 1.25 × nbytes)). Add a Metal regression sort at more than 2 GiB of scratch.

**Prediction after the fix:** S2 at voxel 0.005 settles 0.45–0.5 voxel (2.2–2.5 mm) with no overlap.

### 11.2 The edge leak: mechanism, and whether #4660 covers it

**Mechanism.** `collision_sdf` gives each grid node a single collider: the smallest signed distance over all colliders
(`rasterized_collisions.py`). At the concave line where the rough floor plane meets a frictionless side-wall box,
nodes within about a voxel of the wall take the wall's normal (±y) and its zero friction, and so keep no floor
constraint. Sustained shear then moves particles down through z = 0. Before the blow-up, 5 % of C1's failing
particles were already below the floor (down to −9 mm).

The four variants agree with this:
* rough side walls (C3) leak nothing, because their friction also stops motion along z;
* a box floor (C2) changes the geometry at the junction but still leaks a little;
* without side walls (C4) a single stray particle remains.

**#4660 does not change it.** Its collider hunks replace the averaged face normal used for the distance *sign* with
angle-weighted pseudonormals inside one mesh, with a fallback when they cancel. The one-collider-per-node choice
between meshes is untouched.

**Prediction.** C1 with the variant still goes non-finite. If so, it is a candidate upstream issue: "particles leak
through the concave junction of two colliders when one is frictionless", with C1 (about 135k particles, 8 s) as the
repro. Newton #2697 (particles through *moving* walls, open) is the nearest report. A CUDA cross-check on the L4
stays optional: the code path is device-independent.

**C1 with #4660 is finite, at rest, with no particle below the floor: the variant covers the edge leak.** My 11.2
mechanism (one collider per node) was wrong, and the upstream-issue item is dropped.

What actually happens: the vendored sign test sums the *unweighted* normals of every triangle within 1 mm of the
closest point (`get_average_face_normal`, `_CLOSEST_POINT_NORMAL_EPSILON = 1e-3`). At a box edge the faces contribute
unequal triangle counts, so the sum is biased and some exterior points get an *interior* sign. The side wall's bottom
edge coincides with the floor plane. Points just below or beside it are then read as deep inside the frictionless
wall, the wall wins the per-node minimum, and its gradient points the wrong way.

#4660 replaces that sum with angle-weighted pseudonormals, plus a fallback when they cancel. Its description names this
case: "cancelled collider normals can also give exterior points an interior distance sign". For a P0 run (C1) the
variant's only other difference is how yield parameters are normalised (weight sums instead of particle volume plus
ε). 02c pins it with `pr4660c`, the PR's collider hunks alone.

### 15.2 The heap / layer gap: is it known? (solved elsewhere?)

Same material, same solver, same preset, two flow geometries:
* the plane-shear layer deposits at **36.7°** (atan μ + 2.5°);
* the axisymmetric heap stands at **29.9–30.7°** (atan μ − 3.5 to −4.3°).

That is a 6–7° spread from geometry alone, and neither resolution (+0.8° from R/dx 10 to 20), iterations (+0.02°) nor
the PR's strain fix (+3° at R/dx 20) removes it. Real dry sand's repose and stopping angles differ by only 2–5°
(GDR MiDi 2004, plan section 1).

**Prior art.** No published MPM work reports a Drucker–Prager heap's repose against atan μ; emerge-engine's
explicit-MPM repose gap is also unexplained. What is known:
* Drucker–Prager's strength does not depend on the stress state's Lode angle. Vermeer & de Borst (1984), quoted in
  [Rock Mech. Rock Eng. 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00603-012-0278-2): "useful for stiff clays
  with low friction angles but not for sand".
* Abaqus ([extended DP models](https://classes.engineering.wustl.edu/2009/spring/mase5513/abaqus/docs/v6.6/books/usb/pt05ch18s03abm28.html))
  and MOOSE ([DruckerPrager](https://cardinal.cels.anl.gov/source/userobjects/SolidMechanicsPlasticDruckerPrager.html))
  match DP to Mohr–Coulomb per stress state: outer tip, inner tip, Lode zero, plane strain.
* Klár et al. 2016 ([thesis](http://web.cs.ucla.edu/~dt/theses/klar-thesis.pdf)) fit DP to MC at the compression tips
  and give no repose-versus-φ calibration.

For our cone √J₂ ≤ μp with μ = 0.68, the equivalent Mohr–Coulomb angle is sin φ = μ cos θ / (1 + μ sin θ / √3), so it
depends on the Lode angle θ:

| Stress state | Equivalent MC angle |
|---|---|
| Triaxial compression | 29.5° |
| Pure shear | 42.8° |
| Triaxial extension | 47.1° |

The heap's flank (batch 01 stress export) sits between pure shear and compression. A single μ therefore cannot make
both geometries stop near one sand angle unless the yield surface depends on the Lode angle.

**Verdict.** The gap is the known form limitation of Drucker–Prager: not solved elsewhere for MPM sand, and with no
published calibration. It confirms R3.

**Particles per cell (solved elsewhere?).** Isaac Lab's MPM tuning guide
([tune_mpm](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/release/3.0.0/source/concepts/solver-tuning/tune_mpm.html)) says to
refine `particles_per_cell` until the measured behaviour stops changing; no published footing-settlement ppc study was
found. Our numbers show 8 ppc is not converged for resting contact. At voxel 0.005 it is badly so: 2.1 voxels, and
particles inside the box. *(Inference: with 2 particles per voxel edge, PIC quadrature under-integrates the Q1 velocity
/ P0 strain pairing near the collider, which softens the bed.)*

### 17.1 What the measurements already say about the Lode angle

Drucker–Prager in this solver is √J₂ ≤ μp. The `DB16` dof mapping is orthonormal for the half-Frobenius norm,
so |r_T| = √J₂ and r_N = √(3/2)·p. The equivalent Mohr–Coulomb angle is therefore
sin φ = μ cos θ / (1 + μ sin θ/√3), with θ = +30° at triaxial compression (TC).

The 02d heaps sit at Drucker–Prager's triaxial-compression angle:

| μ | DP angle at TC | Heap, R/dx 20 estimate |
|---|---|---|
| 0.68 | 29.5° | 30.7° |
| 0.90 | 38.2° | 38.4° |

The plane-shear layers sit at intermediate Lode angles, where Drucker–Prager is 7–13° stronger. That
confirms R3: the heap/layer gap is the circular deviatoric section, not the solver.

Equivalent Mohr–Coulomb angle at μ = 0.68, computed by `gc_check.py`, which 03a commits as
`scripts/sand_fidelity/lode/gc_check.py`:

| Surface | TC (θ +30°) | θ +15° | Pure shear (0°) | θ −15° | TE (−30°) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drucker–Prager (μ as √J₂/p) | 29.5 | 36.6 | 42.8 | 47.0 | 47.1 |

### 17.2 Prior art (searched 2026-10-15)

* **The DB16 line uses Drucker–Prager by choice.**
  * Daviet & Bertails-Descoubes 2016:
    [JNNFM](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301719289_Nonsmooth_simulation_of_dense_granular_flows_with_pressure-dependent_yield_stress)
    and [SIGGRAPH](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2897824.2925877).
  * Rousseau et al. 2022, [arXiv 2211.04865](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.04865), the Sand6 validation, say
    "the Drucker-Prager yield surface thus defines a second-order cone … simpler numerical treatment as compared
    to the hexagon-like Mohr-Coulomb model". They also find the collapses match experiments when μ is set to
    the *avalanche* angle, not the stop angle.
  * **No published DB16-style implicit (SOCCP, Gauss–Seidel) MPM with a non-circular deviatoric section was
    found.** This would be new; nothing to port.
* **Mohr–Coulomb is convex and conic-representable, but not as one second-order cone.**
  * Krabbenhøft, Lyamin & Sloan 2007,
    [IJSS](https://www.newcastle.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/22625/88_Formulation-and-solution-of-some-plasticity-problems-as-conic-programs.pdf):
    3D Mohr–Coulomb as conic constraints.
  * Martin & Makrodimopoulos 2008,
    [J. Eng. Mech.](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/abs/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9399%282008%29134%3A4%28339%29):
    semidefinite programming in principal stresses.
  * So convexity survives. The closed-form SOC projection in `solve_sliding_*` (a 1D Newton on the circle) does
    not.
* **Smooth Lode functions.**
  * Abbo & Sloan 1995 (hyperbolic apex) and Abbo, Lyamin, Sloan & Hambleton 2011, a C2 corner rounding,
    [IJSS PDF](https://www.newcastle.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0014/23423/12_A-C2-continuous-approximation-to-the-Mohr-Coulomb-yield-surface.pdf).
    This is what MOOSE's `TensorMechanicsPlasticMohrCoulomb` cites
    ([doc](https://mooseframework.inl.gov/releases/moose/v1.0.0/doxygen/modules/classTensorMechanicsPlasticMohrCoulomb.html)).
  * Lagioia & Panteghini 2016,
    [Proc. R. Soc. A 472:20150713](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspa/article/472/2185/20150713/57683/On-the-existence-of-a-unique-class-of-yield-and):
    one "generalized classical" (GC) Lode function covers Drucker–Prager, Mohr–Coulomb, rounded Mohr–Coulomb,
    Matsuoka–Nakai and Lade–Duncan:
    Γ(θ_s) = α cos[⅓ arccos(−β sin 3θ_s) − γπ/6].
  * Parameters from [arXiv 2106.12860](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.12860) Table 1:
    * Mohr–Coulomb: α = sec((γ̄+1)π/6), β = 1, γ = 1 − γ̄, where γ̄ = (6/π) atan(sin φ/√3).
    * Matsuoka–Nakai: α = (2/3)√A₁·M_c, β = A₂/A₁^{3/2}, γ = 0.
    * Here A₁ = (K−3)/(K−9), A₂ = K/(K−9), K = (9−sin²φ)/(1−sin²φ) and M_c = 6 sin φ/(3−sin φ).
  * **Checked numerically here.** Both rows reproduce the exact Mohr–Coulomb and Matsuoka–Nakai radii at five
    Lode angles to 4 digits (μ = 0.68). The α for Matsuoka–Nakai is (2/3)√A₁·M_c; the A₁ (not √A₁) form in one
    search snippet is wrong.
  * Inner rounding with β < 1 (Lester & Sloan 2018) has a worst φ error of −0.51° at β = 0.999 and −0.16° at
    β = 0.9999, and never exceeds Mohr–Coulomb.
  * The Matsuoka–Nakai convex branch is the arccos/3 form (Panteghini & Lagioia 2014,
    [IJNAMG](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/nag.2228)).
* **How engineering MPM codes return to Mohr–Coulomb.** All use a Mohr–Coulomb yield surface with a different,
  smooth or non-associated, flow potential and an explicit or iterative return:
  * CB-Geo `mpm` [`mohr_coulomb.tcc`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cb-geo/mpm/develop/include/materials/mohr_coulomb.tcc):
    * Mohr–Coulomb plus a tension cutoff, with no rounding of the yield surface;
    * a Menétrey–Willam-like smooth plastic potential;
    * an iterative corrector capped at 100 passes that returns silently when it doesn't converge.
  * Anura3D ([repo](https://github.com/cafvf/Anura3D_OpenSource)) and Solowski–Sloan MPM column collapses:
    Mohr–Coulomb with Sloan-style explicit substepping.
  * Uintah: Arenisca4's J3 dependence is a Lode scaling function with a choice of Mohr–Coulomb or Gudehus
    ([svn log](https://sci.utah.edu/~harman/svnstat/svnstat_2014/dir_CCA_Components_MPM_ConstitutiveModel.html)).
  * FLAC3D ([doc](https://docs.itascasoftware.com/itasca970/common/models/mohr/doc/modelmohr.html)):
    non-associated shear flow.
* **Convergence.** DB16's guarantees (a convex SOCCP, with Gauss–Seidel as block descent) already do not cover
  Newton's zero-dilatancy rheology. Its cone radius μr_N depends on the unknown, a de Saxcé bipotential,
  quasi-variational case, as in Coulomb contact. The solver has no global convergence proof today; every
  parity run hits the 250-iteration cap on Metal and CUDA. A Lode-dependent radius μ(θ(r)) is the same class
  of problem: a projection onto a convex set that depends on the solution. **Expectation: no new theoretical
  guarantee is lost, and none is gained. Convergence is measured, not assumed (17.6 gate G3).**

**Verdict: a GC Lode function, inner-rounded Mohr–Coulomb by default, with Matsuoka–Nakai selectable.**

* **Mohr–Coulomb as the default.** Mohr–Coulomb is the only surface whose friction angle is the same at every
  Lode angle. The infinite-slope repose of a cohesionless Mohr–Coulomb material is φ whatever the
  intermediate stress, so a heap and a plane-shear layer deposit at the same angle, atan μ, by construction.
  That is the owner's acceptance target.
* **Matsuoka–Nakai as the option.** Matsuoka–Nakai is smooth and matches real sand better at intermediate
  stress: true-triaxial sand shows plane-strain φ a few degrees above triaxial φ. But it is up to +4.4° above φ
  between the corners at μ = 0.68, so it predicts a layer steeper than the heap.
* **One GC kernel with three parameter sets.** "drucker_prager" (α = 1, β = 0), "mohr_coulomb" (β = 0.999)
  and "matsuoka_nakai" share one GC kernel.
* **Rejected: Lode-dependent DP scaling with ad-hoc factors.** It is the same kernel with uncalibrated
  constants.
* **Rejected for now: the exact associated D-metric projection onto the GC section.** That is a 6-unknown
  Newton solve per strain node per iteration. It is the fallback, F1, if gate G3 fails.

### 17.3 Kernel design: variant `pr4660` + Lode

* **Generation.** Generated like `pr4660c`: `scripts/vendor_mpm_variant.py`, the `pr4660` diff, then our own
  patch `patches/metalsim/lode-yield-surface.diff`. Two output modules differ only in one `wp.constant`
  (`_LODE_SURFACE`), so the hot loop has no runtime branch:
  * `implicit_mpm_pr4660mc` (Mohr–Coulomb);
  * `implicit_mpm_pr4660mn` (Matsuoka–Nakai).

  The parity path never imports them; the parity tests and the vendor-drift check stay as they are.
* **Lode angle in the right frame.** `solve_local_stress_impl` works in the Delassus eigenbasis, an arbitrary
  orthogonal 5×5 rotation that does not preserve J3. The flow rule therefore gets `rot`:
  1. Rotate the trial stress back with `_local_to_world`.
  2. Build the deviator with `SymmetricTensorMapper.dof_to_value_3d` (DB16 mapping) with dof[0] = 0.
  3. Compute sin 3θ = clamp(−(3√3/2)·J₃/J₂^{3/2}, −1, 1), with the sign pinned by a CPU test (TC vs TE)
     because Newton's stress is compression-positive.
  4. Use k(θ) = 1 when J₂ ≈ 0.
* **Yield radius.** φ = atan(friction), so `friction` keeps its Isaac meaning of "repose ≈ atan μ".
  `yield_stress` is the Mohr–Coulomb cohesion c:
  * ys(θ) = (p sin φ + c cos φ)·Γ-shape(θ), normalised so that at θ = 0 the radius is p sin φ + c cos φ;
  * with φ = 0 this gives √J₂ = c, the shear yield stress τ_y in simple shear, so Bingham and Tresca stay
    consistent;
  * φ → 0 degrades to von Mises (unit-tested);
  * the yield-stress derivative used by the dilatancy path scales by the same k(θ).
* **Lagged-Lode return.**
  1. Pass 1 takes θ from the trial stress and calls the unchanged `solve_sliding_*` with ys(θ).
  2. Pass 2 recomputes θ from the pass-1 stress and solves again.

  The pass count is a compile-time constant (2), so the loop is bounded. The flow direction stays radial in
  the D-metric, so the deviatoric flow is non-associated, as in the engineering MPM codes above.
  `project_stress` (pre/post-processing, world frame) gets the same θ-scaled radius.
* **Cost.** Per local solve: one 3×3 determinant, one arccos, one cos and a second sliding solve. The local
  solve's share of an iteration is unmeasured. Budget ×1.3 per MPM step versus `pr4660`, measured exclusive
  in 03b.
* **CPU unit tests**, Warp on CPU in the sandbox, file `tests/test_mpm_lode.py`:
  1. GC rows equal the exact Mohr–Coulomb and Matsuoka–Nakai radii, φ from 5° to 50°, 61 Lode angles.
  2. The deviatoric section is convex: midpoint test, φ from 5° to 50°.
  3. TC and TE sign: a hand-built uniaxial-compression stress gives the TC radius.
  4. Rotation invariance: θ is the same from world and from rotated-local input, for 1,000 random orthogonal
     5×5 rotations.
  5. Projected random stresses satisfy the Mohr–Coulomb condition (σ₁−σ₃)/(σ₁+σ₃) = sin φ, or I₁I₂/I₃ = K for
     Matsuoka–Nakai, to 1e-4 after 2 passes over random D, b and rot. The fixed-point residual is reported.
  6. "drucker_prager" in the new kernel is bitwise `pr4660`.
  7. φ = 0 gives von Mises with radius c.
  8. Stress inside the surface gives zero plastic flow.

### 17.5 Materials: representability (research first; numbers to be confirmed per batch)

The solver's per-particle model is a capped Drucker–Prager, or GC with this work, plus:

* a pressure-independent `yield_stress`, which is √J₂ ≤ s, i.e. τ_y in simple shear;
* a viscosity term in the local solve;
* a compressive cap (`yield_pressure`, `hardening`) and a tension bound (`tensile_yield_ratio`);
* elasticity (`young_modulus`; the default of 1e15 is rigid-plastic).

With friction 0, yield_stress τ_y and viscosity η, that is a Bingham fluid. The exact viscosity scaling,
τ = τ_y + η·γ̇ or 2η·ε̇, is pinned by a CPU Couette test before any material batch uses it.

### 17.6 Acceptance predictions for the physical preset with Mohr–Coulomb (stated before any run)

* **G3 convergence and cost.**

## 18. Batch 03a as built (`scripts/sand_fidelity/batch_03a.sh`): what changed from section 17, and the CPU facts

Section 17's plan was accepted as written. Building and CPU-testing it changed three details. Each change follows
from a measurement below, not a toggle.

### 18.1 Changes from section 17

* **Corner rounding β = 0.9999, not 0.999.** The rounding error is largest exactly at the triaxial corners, and
  that is where the heap sits (17.1). It also grows with φ.

  | Worst φ error | φ 20° | 30° | 34.2° | 40° | 45° | 50° |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | β 0.999 | −0.24° | −0.42° | −0.51° | −0.66° | −0.82° | −1.01° |
  | β 0.9999 | −0.08° | −0.13° | −0.16° | −0.21° | −0.26° | −0.33° |

  0.999 would bias the gravel heap (μ ≈ 1) by −0.8°, which is 40 % of G1's 2° budget.
* **The Lode factor is solved, not lagged.** The planned 2-pass lag (θ from the trial stress, then from the
  pass-1 stress) left plastic stresses up to 9 % off the surface. On 20,000 random cases with the Delassus diagonal
  in [0.2, 2] (anisotropy 10), only 79 % had a residual ≤ 1e-3.

  The D-metric projection turns the deviator, so θ moves. k must solve k = F(k) = k(θ(stress(k))), a 1-D root.

  | Method, anisotropy 10 | Solves | Max residual |
  |---|---|---|
  | Fixed point | 6 | 7e-3 |
  | Fixed point | 10 | 6e-4 |
  | Secant | 4 | 3e-4 |
  | Secant | 6 | 2e-5 |

  At anisotropy 100, the plain secant still left about 1 in 3,000 cases stuck. It overshot past the elastic
  boundary and returned an elastic, too-large stress. The kernel therefore brackets the root:
  * g(k) = F(k) − k is ≥ 0 at the smallest k over all Lode angles and ≤ 0 at the largest;
  * the smallest is mid-face for Mohr–Coulomb (tan θ = −sin φ/√3), not at triaxial extension;
  * secant steps fall back to bisection whenever they leave the bracket;
  * there are at most 8 anisotropic solves (`_LODE_MAX_SOLVES`), with early exit at |g| ≤ 1e-5·k;
  * an elastic trial stress costs one solve, the same as `pr4660`.

  Result: max residual 1.0e-5 at anisotropy 10, and 5.2e-4 at anisotropy 100 (p99 9e-6).

  The cost is up to 8× the local solve on yielding nodes, and is not yet measured. It is G3's timing arm (03b-3).
  Its budget stays ×1.3 per step versus `pr4660`. If that is exceeded, the next step is to measure the real
  Delassus anisotropy and the solve counts on the GPU, not to loosen the tolerance.
* **The Couette test became a decay test.** `mpm:particle_stress` is the stress projected onto the yield surface,
  so it never shows the viscous part: a uniform simple shear reads σ_xz = τ_y at any η and γ̇. The viscosity
  convention is therefore measured from the decay rate of a free-block shear mode v_x = A cos(πz/L).

### 18.2 Material conventions, measured (full solver, Warp CPU, parity and `pr4660mc` identical)

* **`yield_stress` is τ_y in simple shear.** The interior σ_xz is −10.0 Pa for yield_stress 10, at η 0 and 10 and
  γ̇ 2 and 4.
* **The solver's stress is compression-positive (minus Cauchy).** This is the sign the Lode kernel assumes
  (triaxial compression = +30°), now pinned at the solver level too.
* **`viscosity` acts as τ = η·γ̇/2 (= η·ε̇).**
  * The decay rate is 0.469 × ηk²/ρ (η 50, ρ 1,000, dt 1 ms, cell 0.05 m).
  * It is 0.453 at half the cell size and 0.476 at twice the dt.
  * The inviscid background is 0.6 % of it.
  * Consequence: a Bingham plastic viscosity η_pl is `viscosity = 2·η_pl`.
  * The mud and cement presets (05a, 05c) must use the factor 2, and the GUIDE must say so.
  * Section 17.5's typical-parameter column lists η_pl.

### 18.4 Finding: two solver copies in one process collide (owner: this workstream; root cause to confirm)

* **Symptom.** Running the vendored parity solver and any variant in one process, both with the particle-strain
  update, fails Warp code generation. The update runs for warmstart "particles", compliant or hardening material.
  The error is "Couldn't find function overload for 'update_particle_strains_…'".
* **It predates this work.** It reproduces with parity + `pr4660`.
* **Suspected cause.** warp.fem's integrand handling keys on the integrand's name, and the two packages define the
  same names.
* **Production impact: none known.** One process runs one variant: the probes, scenes and batches.
* **Mitigation in the tests.** The tests run each full-solver measurement in its own process.
* **Owner and next step.** Owner: sand fidelity. A minimal reproducer against warp.fem goes in the scene batch
  (04), before two scenes with different fidelities can share a process.
