2. Newton PR #4291 (stress-transpose normalization, Delassus majorization, plastic-strain reconstruction). It is a

## 2. Why the repose angle is low: evidence and hypotheses

**New evidence from the existing runs** (on `origin/main`, never commented on). The three heap estimators disagree by
more than the protocol's own 2° limit:

| voxel (R/dx) | fit, 25–75 % of height | cone volume, atan(3V/πr_toe³) | height/flank |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 (5) | 25.4 | 30.2 | 25.2 |
| 0.005 (10) | 26.3 | 31.5 | 26.4 |
| 0.0033 (15) | 27.2 | 32.8 | 28.0 |
| 0.0025 (20) | 27.8 | **33.3** | 27.8 |

At R/dx 20: h_max 38 mm, toe 82 mm, apex plateau 10 mm. The material volume (3.9e-4 m³) is ~30 % more than a cone
with the fitted slope holds (~3.0e-4 m³), and more than a frustum with that plateau (3.0e-4 m³). So the heap is
**convex**: a rounded crest with a steeper lower flank. The fit window, 25–75 % of a heap only 15 cells tall, sits
largely in the rounded part. The cone-volume angle is within 1° of atan μ = 34.2° and rises in step with the fit.
*(Inference: the bulk of the heap may already stand near atan μ, and the deficit may be a near-surface skin a few
cells thick.)*

Things that are **not** the cause, from the measurements and the code:
* Numerical dissipation. More damping stops flow earlier and steepens heaps, so it works the other way. The lift-speed
  plateau and 4× iterations (+0.3°) rule out under-convergence.
* Elasticity: E = 1e15 makes the material rigid.
* `air_drag`: it is added to a density-scaled node mass (~1,500), and drag × dt ≈ 0.008, i.e. ~5e-6 relative
  (`implicit_mpm_solver_kernels.py` `free_velocity`, `integrate_mass`).
* The unilateral offset: inactive with `critical_fraction = 0`.
* Floor friction: 1.0, above μ.

Ranked hypotheses (cheapest test first):

* **R1. Measurement and size effect (convex heap).** The fitted flank is the rounded crest. The slope deep in the heap
  approaches atan μ.
  *Test:* save h(r) per sector and fit the slope as a function of local depth in cells. Rerun with a heap twice as
  tall in cells (fill 10 cm at voxel 0.005). Pure analysis, ~2 min of GPU.
* **R2. Weak free-surface skin from the discretization.** P0 strain (one stress per cell) with Q1 velocity, at
  partially filled surface cells: stress and pressure are cell averages, so a 1–2 cell skin can shear at low
  confinement. This fits the ~+1° per refinement trend and the convex crest.
  *Test (config only):* `velocity_basis="B2"`, `strain_basis="Q1d"`/`"P1d"`, `integration_scheme="gimp"`, and
  27 → 64 particles per cell, at R/dx 10. Newton's draft [#4660](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/pull/4660)
  (2026-10-13, gdaviet) fixes P1d/Q1d strain modes that "can lose valid strain modes when their first moments cancel"
  in partially filled cells. If P1d/Q1d help but look noisy, that PR is the reason.
* **R3. Drucker–Prager stress-state geometry.** The yield cone is circular in the deviatoric plane: strength depends on
  the intermediate stress (Lode angle), unlike Mohr–Coulomb (see the DP/MC comparisons
  [Springer 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00603-012-0278-2) and
  [Reuleaux plasticity](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1158468/reuleaux-plasticity-improving-mohr-coulomb-and-drucker-prager)).
  A radially spreading, axisymmetric heap has hoop extension. With a coaxial flow rule this puts σ_θθ below p, raises
  J2, and makes the flank yield before τ/σ_n reaches μ. *(Inference.)*
  Scale of the effect: the MC angle that matches this cone in triaxial compression, sin φ = 3μ/(2√3 + μ), gives
  18.1 / 29.5 / 38.2° for μ = 0.4 / 0.68 / 0.9. Linear extrapolation of the fit to zero voxel lands near ~29–30° at
  μ = 0.68.
  *Test:* G1. A plane-shear layer should stop at atan μ if the rheology is right. If G1 passes and the cone stays low,
  the model is the cause. Also export `mpm:particle_stress` from the heap and plot sqrt(J2)/(μp) and the Lode angle
  along the flank.
* **R4. Solver defects fixed upstream after 1.5.2** ([#4277](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/issues/4277) /
  [#4291](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/pull/4291): a factor ½ in `_symmetric_part_transposed_op`, still
  present in our vendored copy and in the 1.7-dev install; Delassus diagonal majorization that "accounts for
  spherical/deviatoric coupling that the solver had been dropping"; plastic-strain reconstruction). For a rigid,
  cohesionless cone the converged solution is invariant to the stress scale (the cone is scale-free; yield_pressure is
  1e12, never reached), and 4× iterations changed nothing. So the expected effect is ≤ 0.5°. It is still worth
  backporting in the physically-right mode, because it changes convergence and anything with cohesion or elasticity.
  *Test:* a backport on a branch, at R/dx 10.

**What fixes it, depending on the outcome:**
* R1 / R2 → configuration: basis, resolution, ppc, plus a properly built repose measurement.
* R3 → either (a) calibrate μ per target material against G2 (config only; the incline then stands steeper than the
  heap, by the same few degrees), or (b) a code change: a Lode-angle-dependent yield surface (Mohr–Coulomb-like or
  Matsuoka–Nakai) in the rheology's local projection. (b) is no longer a second-order cone, so the DB16 local solver
  needs a new projection. It is substantial and only justified if (a) breaks G1 or G4.

## 3. Coupling fidelity

**C1. Excess drop penetration is the one-step exchange lag.** Our own data: lagged mode, 50 iterations,
v_impact = 1.716 m/s. Peak penetration 38.8 mm at dt 1/120 and 31.7 mm at dt 1/240: a difference of 7.1 mm, against
v·Δdt = 1.716 × (1/120 − 1/240) = 7.15 mm. In lagged mode the sand's decelerating impulse reaches MuJoCo one step late,
so the body travels about one extra step at speed. Extrapolated to dt → 0: ~25 mm at 50 iterations, ~16 mm for the
converged run (29.8 mm at 1/120). Both are inside the 10–25 mm band. The caveat is that this rests on two points, at
different solve settings.
*Fixes:*
* (a) A smaller coupled dt: config only, cost ∝ 1/dt.
* (b) Newton's proxy relaxation iterations (`SolverCoupledProxy.Config.iterations`, `proxy_relaxation_mode="aitken"`).
  They are in 1.5.2 and 1.7-dev (`.venv/.../coupled/solver_coupled_proxy.py:293, 1324`) and re-run both solvers over
  the same interval until the exchanged force is consistent. This is a code change in `MPMCoupling`; the cost is
  ≈ iterations × the coupled step.
* (c) Long term: a monolithic solve. Newton's `SolverCoupledADMM` is documented only for MJWarp + VBD; MPM support is
  unknown.

**C2. Half-voxel resting settlement is grid contact resolution.** It is "sub-voxel gaps" per gdaviet on #3473.
Collider nodes are active within ~0.25 voxel (S2), with velocity-level contact and no positional correction.
Expected ∝ voxel. The voxel-0.01 rest run (19.7 mm) used 50 iterations, so it is not evidence against this. Converged
reruns at voxel 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.005 are needed.
*Options:* `collider_basis="pic27"` (recommended in #1251), `project_outside_colliders` (Isaac's particle-level
push-out), and finer voxels near contacts.

**C3. Creep follows solve convergence** (measured). The physically-right mode runs a converged solve: tolerance 1e-6,
max_iterations ≥ 250, residual logged.

**C4.** Once C1 is removed, the remaining drop depth should follow Uehara with μ = tan(repose measured in G2). If the
material is effectively weak (R3), the depth will be too deep by the ratio of the tangents: tan 34°/tan 27.8° ≈ 1.3.

Other coupling literature, for the design of (b)/(c):
* [Hu et al. 2018 MLS-MPM/CPIC](https://yuanming.taichi.graphics/publication/2018-mlsmpm/): impulse-based two-way
  coupling, explicit.
* [Gast/Schroeder penalty coupling 2017](https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~craigs/papers/2017-rigid-mpm/paper.pdf): tight
  coupling; shows a cube coming to rest in sand.
* Daviet & Bertails-Descoubes 2016 ([HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01310189)): rigid coupling inside the
  nonsmooth solve.
* [Yu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05046v1): convex, weakly coupled, asynchronous.
* [Daviet 2026 Mixed MPM](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/prl/mixed_mpm/): Newton's current solver family, with
  two-way rigid coupling demos. I could not read the PDF here (over the fetch size limit); its project page reports no
  repose or collapse validation.

## 5. Recommendation

### Benchmarks and pass criteria (physically-right mode)

| Bench | Pass |
|---|---|
| B1 tilting box (G1) | θ_fail within ±1° of atan μ for μ = 0.4 / 0.68 / 0.9 at ≥ 10 cells depth; ±2° at 5 cells |
| B2 repose (G2) | fit, cone-volume and height/flank estimators agree within 2°; angle within 2° of the target material's repose (dry sand 30–35°, chosen per preset); heap angle in [θ_stop(B1), θ_stop + 3°]; monotone and converging with R/dx, with the last refinement < 0.5° |
| B3 column collapse (G3) | 2D runout exponents 1.0 ± 0.1 (a ≤ 2) and 0.67 ± 0.07 (a ≥ 3); prefactors within 15 % of Lajeunesse 2005 / Lube 2005 for the calibrated μ |
| B4 sphere drop (G4) | d within 15 % of Uehara's law at h = 0.1–0.8 m, using μ = tan(B2 angle); fitted exponent of H 0.33 ± 0.05; d varies < 10 % between dt 1/240 and 1/480 |
| B5 footing (G5) | rest settlement ≤ 0.25 voxel and ≤ 3 mm, creep < 0.1 mm/s; bearing failure load within a factor of 2 of Vesic for φ = B2 angle; failure for μ ≤ 0.3 |

### Hypotheses, cheapest first

1. R1: measurement and size effect. Analysis plus one taller heap.
2. C1: exchange lag. A dt sweep (config); then proxy iterations (small code change).
3. R2: discretization skin. Basis, ppc, gimp (config).
4. R3: Drucker–Prager geometry. B1 vs B2, plus a stress export (config plus a probe).
5. C2: contact resolution. Voxel sweep converged, pic27, project-outside (config).
6. R4: backport #4291 (code, flag-gated).
7. Only if needed: a Lode-dependent yield surface (code, large), or a monolithic coupling.

### Physically-right mode vs parity mode

Parity mode stays bitwise Isaac:
* vendored 1.5.2 copy;
* staggered exchange for Franka-Pour;
* Isaac's solver settings;
* the parity tests unchanged.

The physically-right mode is a named `MPMConfig` preset (e.g. `fidelity="physical"`):
* **Config only:**
  * lagged exchange;
  * converged solve (tol 1e-6, iterations ≥ 250, residual logged);
  * coupled dt ≤ 1/480 near impacts, or MPM substeps with per-substep exchange;
  * voxel ≤ 0.01 under contacts;
  * whatever basis or ppc R2 selects;
  * calibrated μ per material preset.
* **Code, flag-gated:**
  * proxy relaxation iterations / Aitken in `MPMCoupling` (port from `SolverCoupledProxy`);
  * the #4291 backport as a kernel variant (the generated vendored copy stays byte-identical; the variant lives beside
    it and is selected by the flag);
  * new probes (tilting box, sphere drop, profile and stress export).
* **Only if R3 is confirmed and calibration is not enough:** a Lode-dependent yield surface.

## 6. First GPU batch (description only; no script yet)

"sand_fidelity_01". Everything except item 5 uses existing code paths or new probe files; there are no solver changes.
Total ≈ 45–60 min on the 16-inch.

1. **Repose diagnostics (R1, R3).** μ 0.68 at R/dx 10 and 20, and a fill twice as tall at R/dx 10. Save per-sector
   h(r) profiles and, at rest, particle positions plus `mpm:particle_stress`. Then compute the local slope vs depth in
   cells, and sqrt(J2)/(μp) and the Lode angle on the flank.
2. **Tilting box (R3, B1).** μ 0.4 / 0.68 / 0.9 at voxel 0.005, plus μ 0.68 at voxel 0.01. Record θ_fail (first
   sustained surface speed > 1 mm/s) and the post-avalanche angle.
3. **Discretization variants (R2).** Repose at R/dx 10, μ 0.68, varying one setting each from the baseline:
   `velocity_basis` B2, `strain_basis` Q1d, P1d, `integration_scheme` gimp, and 64 particles per cell.
4. **Drop dt sweep (C1).** Box drop from 0.15 m, lagged mode, converged solve (250 iterations, tolerance 1e-6), voxel
   0.02, at dt 1/120, 1/240, 1/480, 1/960. Repeat dt 1/480 at voxel 0.01. Check penetration against d₀ + v·dt.
5. **Rest voxel sweep (C2).** Converged rest test at voxel 0.02 / 0.01 / 0.005, with S2 and pic27, each with and
   without a `project_outside` call after each MPM step.
6. **Sphere drop (B4) smoke test.** D = 0.05 m at h = 0.2 m and dt 1/480, so the fixture is ready for the full
   Uehara sweep in batch 02.

Batch 02 depends on the outcome of batch 01:
* proxy iterations in `MPMCoupling` (C1 fix b);
* the #4291 kernel-variant backport;
* the full Uehara sweep;
* a column-collapse re-check with the calibrated μ;
* then the `fidelity="physical"` preset with its tests. The parity tests must stay bitwise.

### 8.1 The solve never reaches its tolerance: "converged" means capped at 250 iterations

logged: `MPMCoupling` installs `newton_compat` with `log=False` and captures the step.)*

**Solved elsewhere?** No report. Isaac Lab's default (250 iterations, 1e-4) would sit at the cap too.

**CUDA already shows it.** The L4 parity records (`runs/parity/isaaclab3_mpm*/**/solves.npz`, recorded with

### 8.2 Tilting box: θ_stop is not established; what was measured is creep onset

The detector fired below atan μ in every run (μ 0.68: 33.2° against 34.2°; μ 0.9: 41.2° against 42.0°; μ 0.68 at

### 8.3 μ 0.4: a real blow-up, with an edge leak before it

### 8.4 Resting box: S2 at grid resolution; pic27 is worse; pic27 with project_outside is broken by construction

S2 without projection settles 10.2 mm (0.51 voxel) at voxel 0.02 and 4.5 mm (0.45 voxel) at voxel 0.01. The box does
not overlap the particles (40 particles, 0.6 mm deep at voxel 0.02; none at voxel 0.01), and project_outside changes

sub-voxel, as gdaviet describes in #3473. Creep is 0.4 mm/s, which fails B5's 0.1 mm/s.

pic27 is worse at every voxel: 13.7 / 11.3 / 23.0 mm, creep 1.7–7.2 mm/s, rising with resolution.

The data already show why pic27 behaves this way, with no new run needed:
* In the first 0.05 s both pic27 runs fall freely 11.9 mm (free fall over 0.05 s is 12.3 mm) with zero force. S2
  catches the box at 2.4 mm.
* A pic collider engages only once particles overlap the body. Then it kicks: F/W 5.4, the box bounces, F = 0 for

**Solved elsewhere?**
* #1251 recommends pic27 for a G1 walking on sand at coarse voxels. The same thread reports "contact force from mpm

* Isaac Lab refuses project_outside inside coupled entries (`coupler.py`).
* Verdict: pic27 is not the fix for resting bodies here, and pic27 with project_outside is an invalid combination, not

The ring-heave metric (−14 to −36 mm) is contaminated. The bearing bed has no walls, so its edges slump. A drop of
this size is not heave.

### 8.5 Voxel 0.005 with S2: a Warp-fork allocator limit, not inherent to S2

The crash happens in S2 collider rasterisation (`_rasterize_colliders` → `fem.interpolate` → `bsr_set_from_triplets` →
`radix_sort_pairs`). The Metal fork's sort allocates its scratch with `scratch_ptr(nbytes)` as a **uint8** array of
`max(nbytes, 2 × previous)` elements. The failed request was exactly 3 GiB (3,145,729,280 bytes): double a previous
1.5 GiB buffer. Two defects:
1. the element count is in bytes, so it hits the int32 element limit at 2 GiB;
2. doubling overshoots what the sort needs.

pic27 at the same 1.57M particles ran, because it builds no S2 interpolation matrix.

It is the same *class* as the deterministic-plan crash, a Warp array limit, but **not the same allocator**:
* That crash ("3.5 G elements", MuJoCo Warp's fused update in deterministic mode) comes from `deterministic.py`'s
  scatter record buffers, sized as launch dim × records per thread and checked against
  `DETERMINISTIC_SCATTER_MAX_CAPACITY`. That is a record count above int32, not the byte-sized sort scratch.
* So P2 does not cover it. The deterministic path's own `uint8` sort-reduce workspace
  (`wp_deterministic_sort_reduce_workspace_size`) is a separate allocation and could hit the byte form of this bug too
  at larger sizes; worth checking when that work resumes.

**Fix (prerequisite P2, Warp fork):** allocate the scratch in 8-byte elements, and grow to at most
max(nbytes, min(2 × previous, 1.25 × nbytes)). Add a Metal regression sort at more than 2 GiB of scratch.

**Prediction after the fix:** S2 at voxel 0.005 settles 0.45–0.5 voxel (2.2–2.5 mm) with no overlap.

### 8.6 Sphere drop: the largest gap, with a confounded bed

The sphere penetrated 60.4 mm against Uehara's 16.3 mm at μ 0.7, and the ball ended fully buried.

How it decelerated: from 2.0 to 1.2 m/s in 4 steps (peak 10.6 g), then only about 2.4 g. The mean stopping force
was 7 N:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Energy to absorb, ½mv² + mgd | 0.42 J |
| Depth over which it was absorbed | 0.060 m |
| Mean stopping force (measured) | 7 N |
| Mean force if Uehara held (stopping at 16 mm) | 26 N |

The lag v·dt = 4 mm explains almost none of the gap.

There is also a confounder: the bed (0.2 × 0.2 × 0.12 m) has **no walls**. As a free column of aspect about 1.2, it
collapses on the impact timescale (√(H/g) ≈ 0.11 s, stop at 0.18 s). Lube et al. 2004 find an undisturbed central
cone of about 59° in every collapse, which here leaves only about 2.8 cm undisturbed at the top centre. That is
smaller than the crater, so part of the measured penetration may be bed subsidence, and the slumping bed removes
lateral confinement around the impact.

**Solved elsewhere?** No MPM report against Uehara or Katsuragi–Durian was found. Agarwal et al. 2019 validate
continuum plasticity for wheels, not impacts.
