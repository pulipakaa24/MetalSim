# Cross-platform floating-point determinism and simulator parity (survey, as of 2026-10-10)

Context: metalsim runs NVIDIA Newton/Warp implicit MPM on Apple Metal and wants parity with Isaac Lab on CUDA. Below, "verified" means I read the primary source (doc page, source file, or changelog) during this session. Source dates are given where available.

## Q1. What do game/physics engines with cross-platform determinism actually do (FMA, fast-math, transcendentals, reduction order, GPU)?

### Takeaway
Every engine that claims cross-platform bitwise determinism uses the same CPU-only recipe: (1) disable FP contraction (`-ffp-contract=off`), (2) no fast-math (`/fp:precise` or `-ffp-model=precise`), (3) replace libm transcendentals (sin/cos/atan2) with the engine's own polynomial code, (4) fix every ordering (contacts follow creation order, deterministic sorts/hashes, no order taken from thread completion), (5) pin FPU state (round-to-nearest, consistent FTZ/DAZ). They rely on IEEE-754 correct rounding of + − × ÷ sqrt only. None of them claims GPU cross-vendor determinism. Each verifies determinism with a hash or rewind check in CI.

### Cited Findings
- **Box2D v3** (Erin Catto, "Determinism", 2024-08-27): disables FMA with `-ffp-contract=off` on Clang/GCC; says to "never use" fast-math (no meaningful speedup on MSVC, lower accuracy); replaces `sinf`/`cosf`/`atan2f` with custom approximations because `atan2f` differed across platforms, and keeps the custom sin/cos even though the libm versions matched in tests, because the author does not trust that to hold; relies on `sqrtf` (compiled to the `sqrtss` instruction) being correctly rounded — [Box2D blog](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/determinism/)
- Box2D ordering: atomics and mutexes don't make ordering deterministic, because thread completion order varies. Contact order follows body/shape creation order, and per-thread bitsets are merged with a bitwise OR, which gives a deterministic order. A "Falling Hinges" test runs in GitHub Actions on every PR and checks a sleep step and a transform hash (0x5e70e5fe) across MSVC/Clang/GCC on x64/ARM. A video shows Apple M2 and AMD Ryzen producing identical results. Reported performance cost: none noticeable. — [Box2D blog](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/determinism/)
- **Jolt Physics** (current docs): the CMake option `CROSS_PLATFORM_DETERMINISTIC` makes the library about 8% slower. Required flags: `-ffp-model=precise` (clang) or `/fp:precise` (MSVC), plus `-ffp-contract=off`. Round-to-nearest and consistent FTZ/DAZ across threads. Jolt's `Sin`/`Cos`, `QuickSort` (instead of `std::sort`), `BinaryHeapPush/Pop` and `Hash` replace the std versions, which differ across platforms. Same source and same defines everywhere (no mixing `JPH_DOUBLE_PRECISION` builds). Verified on MSVC x86/x64, macOS ARM64, Linux x86-64, ARM32/64, RISC-V, PowerPC, LoongArch and WASM. The docs call this feature "less tested". Broadphase queries, listener callback order and `GetActiveBodies` order remain nondeterministic. Testing uses a "Check Determinism" mode that records state, rewinds, re-steps and compares. — [Jolt docs, Deterministic Simulation section](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)
- **Rapier**: cross-platform determinism requires the `enhanced-determinism` feature, a platform that strictly follows IEEE 754-2008, identical initial conditions, and nalgebra `ComplexField`/`RealField` functions for trig. The feature is incompatible with `simd8`. — [Rapier determinism guide](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/determinism/)
- In Rapier's `Cargo.toml`, `enhanced-determinism = ["simba/libm_force", "parry3d/enhanced-determinism"]`, i.e. it forces the pure-Rust `libm` for transcendentals. The changelog says the feature can now be combined with `parallel` with "results bitwise identical for any thread-pool size", at no measurable cost, resting on "glam's shared scalar core plus libm-pinned transcendentals". It also says contact impulses are stored with canonicalized signed zeros "removing a source of cross-platform divergence". Cross-platform reproducibility still requires matching numeric features (`block-solver`, f32 vs f64). — [rapier Cargo.toml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dimforge/rapier/master/crates/rapier3d/Cargo.toml), [rapier CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dimforge/rapier/master/CHANGELOG.md)
- **Gaffer On Games**, "Floating Point Determinism" (Glenn Fiedler, 2010-02-24): cross-machine determinism is "yes, if…": same compiler, same architecture, same settings. Arbitrary C/C++ float code is not portable across compilers, architectures or even debug/release builds. Practitioner anecdotes: Gas Powered Games set FPU precision and rounding at startup and asserted it every tick; Pandemic Studios found AMD and Intel differed on transcendentals and wrapped them in non-optimized functions; Shawn Hargreaves found that a compiler update broke old replays. — [Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/)
- Same recipe from other domains: `-ffp-contract=off` plus `-ffp-model=precise` gave bit-identical output across several compilers and platforms in a reproducible-RNG library paper (Randompack, arXiv 2605.05099, 2026). — [arXiv 2605.05099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05099) (snippet seen via search; not opened in full)

### Inferences
- The FMA problem in these engines is that contraction is *optional*: the same source can compile to fused or unfused operations depending on compiler and flags. Their answer is to forbid fusion everywhere rather than to require it everywhere, because x86 targets without FMA exist. For metalsim, Metal and CUDA both have hardware FMA, so the other option, explicit `fma()` everywhere with contraction off, is available. Either way, the requirement is that both sides make the *same* choice at every site.
- None of these engines covers PhysX, Havok or Bullet with documented cross-platform guarantees in the sources I read. Those are a gap (below).
- None of these engines attempts GPU determinism. Box2D and Jolt are CPU-only.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary documentation for PhysX (CPU "enhanced determinism" flag), Havok or Bullet cross-platform determinism. From memory, PhysX's `eENABLE_ENHANCED_DETERMINISM` targets same-platform, same-binary determinism under varying scene insertion order, not cross-platform determinism. This is unverified here.
- I could not run the local Metal compiler (`xcrun metal` is not installed on this machine: "unable to find utility metal"). So I could not confirm whether a `-ffp-contract`-style flag exists for MSL. See Q2.

## Q2. GPU reproducibility: reproducible reductions, deterministic atomics, rounding-pinned intrinsics, cross-vendor bitwise matching

### Takeaway
On GPUs, the industry guarantee is at most "same toolkit, same architecture, same SM count" (cuBLAS). Reproducibility across architectures needs a fixed reduction order (sort-then-segmented-reduce) or exact or binned accumulators (ReproBLAS, long accumulators). NVIDIA Warp 1.15 (2026-07) shipped exactly this, as `DeterministicMode.RUN_TO_RUN` and `GPU_TO_GPU`. FMA on CUDA can be pinned with `__fmul_rn`/`__fadd_rn` (never fused) or `--fmad=false`. Warp exposes the latter as the `fuse_fp` module option, which defaults to True, i.e. CUDA kernels *are* contracted by default and CPU kernels use LLVM `FPOpFusion::Standard`. I found no published case of anyone matching CUDA bit-for-bit on another GPU vendor for a non-trivial simulation. Vendor libraries explicitly disclaim cross-device reproducibility.

### Cited Findings
- **cuBLAS 13.4**: "all cuBLAS API routines from a given toolkit version generate the same bit-wise results at every run when executed on GPUs with the same architecture and the same number of SMs". The guarantee breaks with multiple streams unless per-stream workspaces or handles are used. `cublasSetAtomicsMode()` alternatives (e.g. symv/hemv) are "not guaranteed to be bit-wise reproducible because atomics are used". — [cuBLAS docs §2.1.4](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cublas/index.html#results-reproducibility) (page v13.4, 2026)
- **NVIDIA "Floating Point and IEEE 754 Compliance for NVIDIA GPUs"** (written 2011; page last updated 2026-09-13): operations written with explicit-rounding intrinsics "will not be merged into fused multiply-add instructions". FMA merging can also be disabled with a compiler flag. When comparing to CPU results, it advises using SSE rather than x87. — [NVIDIA floating-point whitepaper](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/floating-point/index.html)
- **Warp `fuse_fp`**: module option docs say "fuse_fp: Enable floating-point contraction (FMA fusion) during compilation, defaults to True". `fast_math` defaults to False. — [warp/_src/context.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/warp/main/warp/_src/context.py)
- In the source, CUDA (NVRTC) gets `--fmad=true` or `--fmad=false` from `fuse_fp`, and `--use_fast_math` only if `fast_math` is set. — [warp/native/warp.cu](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/warp/main/warp/native/warp.cu). The CPU (LLVM/Clang) path sets `AllowFPOpFusion = llvm::FPOpFusion::Standard` when `fuse_fp`, otherwise `Strict`. — [warp/native/clang/clang.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/warp/main/warp/native/clang/clang.cpp)
- The option was added in Warp 1.6.0 (2025-02-03): "Add per-module option to disable fused floating point operations, use `wp.set_module_options({"fuse_fp": False})`". — [Warp CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/warp/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- **Warp deterministic mode** (1.15.0, 2026-07-07): `wp.config.deterministic` or the per-module `"deterministic"` option. `RUN_TO_RUN` gives "bit-exact repeatable results on the same GPU". `GPU_TO_GPU` aims to "also target consistency across GPU architectures". It covers two atomic patterns, (1) float accumulation into array elements and (2) atomic counters for slot allocation, and extends to generated backward-pass reductions. — [Warp CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/warp/main/CHANGELOG.md)
- **Mechanism**: kernels write scatter records, then CUB radix sort by destination, then reduce. In `RUN_TO_RUN` "the internal reduction tree may vary across architectures". `GPU_TO_GPU` "launches a segmented reduction kernel that walks each sorted destination segment in thread-index order", a fixed accumulation order that is "more conservative and can be slower". The docs recommend `np.testing.assert_array_equal()` for bit-exact tests. — [Warp docs: Deterministic Execution](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/warp/main/docs/user_guide/execution_and_performance/deterministic_execution.rst)
- **ReproBLAS / binned summation** (Demmel, Nguyen, Ahrens): the result is reproducible regardless of summation order, using a "binned" accumulator that splits the exponent range into fixed-width bins (e.g. W=13 bits for fp32, K=3 folds). About 7n flops, one read-only pass and one parallel reduction. The error bound can be up to 229 times smaller than recursive summation for ill-conditioned double inputs. Key refs: Demmel & Nguyen, "Parallel Reproducible Summation", IEEE TC 64(7), July 2015; UCB/EECS-2015-229 and 2016-121 tech reports. — [Berkeley tech report EECS-2016-121](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2016/EECS-2016-121.html), [ReproBLAS SIAM AN13 slides](https://bebop.cs.berkeley.edu/reproblas/docs/talks/SIAM_AN13.pdf)
- Collange, Defour et al., "Reproducible floating-point atomic addition in data-parallel environment": they fix the order of accumulation or use long (exact) accumulators to give correctly rounded results. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/300338478_Reproducible_floating-point_atomic_addition_in_data-parallel_environment)
- **Intel oneMKL CNR**: GPU Conditional Numerical Reproducibility since 2024.1, but results "may differ across devices (between CPU and GPU or between different kinds of GPUs)". Two GPUs count as the same only if they share a product name. — [Intel CNR page (archived)](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/archive/training/conditional-numerical-reproducibility-cnr.html)
- Bit-reproducible portable HPC (ETH, IPDPS paper): compilers' FMA use is not dictated by the language standards, and vendor math library code is device-specific. Their approach is a portable math library plus control of contraction. — [Designing Bit-Reproducible Portable High-Performance Applications (ETH)](http://htor.inf.ethz.ch/sec/bitrep-ipdps.pdf)
- OP2 (2024): bitwise reproducibility for unstructured-mesh codes using graph colouring, so the result is independent of partitioning or ordering. — [Applied Sciences 14(2):639](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/2/639)
- **Metal**: `MTLCompileOptions.fastMathEnabled=false` corresponds to `mathMode = .safe` plus `mathFloatingPointFunctions = .precise`. In `safe` mode the compiler must not apply transformations that could affect results. — [Apple MTLMathMode docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlmathmode.md), [MTLCompileOptions.mathMode](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlcompileoptions/mathmode.md)
- A developer on Apple's forums reads the MSL spec as requiring correctly rounded fp32 add/sub/mul/recip/div/sqrt/rsqrt/fma with fast math off. Per the same post, the spec still permits FP32 denormal flush-to-zero, and the rounding mode cannot be selected or queried. This is a secondary source to check against the MSL spec. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 844598](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/844598)
- The shift from vendor ML libraries to cross-silicon "kernel contracts" and bitwise-behaviour enforcement is an active 2026 research topic (e.g. "Taming Bitwise Behavior in GPU Kernels with Tensor Core", arXiv 2609.11356; "Kernel Contracts…", arXiv 2604.22032). — [arXiv 2609.11356](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11356), [arXiv 2604.22032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22032) (titles seen in search, not read)

### Inferences
- For metalsim, matching Warp-on-CUDA bit-for-bit requires matching at least: (a) contraction decisions per expression (Warp CUDA default is `--fmad=true`, so NVRTC chooses which a*b+c to fuse, and Metal's compiler independently chooses its own). The robust fix is to compile the CUDA reference with `fuse_fp=False` and also forbid contraction on Metal, or to emit explicit `fma()` on both sides. (b) Transcendental implementations: CUDA libdevice `sinf/expf/powf` versus Metal precise functions, which are not guaranteed to be identical; the fix is shared polynomial implementations, as Box2D and Jolt do. (c) Reduction and atomic order: Warp `GPU_TO_GPU` defines a thread-index-order segmented reduce that a Metal port could reproduce exactly. (d) Denormal and FTZ behaviour. (e) Library calls: cuBLAS, CUB and cuSolverDx/mathdx tile ops have internal orders that Metal would need to replicate.
- Isaac Lab's CUDA reference will by default *not* run with `fuse_fp=False` or a deterministic mode. So even a perfect Metal port cannot match an unmodified Isaac Lab run bitwise: the reference itself is non-deterministic run-to-run wherever float atomics occur (MPM P2G scatter is a classic float-atomic pattern).
- Warp `GPU_TO_GPU` is the closest existing thing to an "architecture-independent order" spec. It is NVIDIA-only but algorithmically portable.

### Gaps
- I found no paper or project showing a non-trivial simulation matching CUDA bit-for-bit on AMD (ROCm/HIP), Intel or Apple GPUs. Vendor docs (cuBLAS, oneMKL CNR) explicitly scope guarantees to the same device model or architecture.
- I did not read Shanmugavelu et al., "Impacts of Floating-Point Non-Associativity on Reproducibility for HPC and Deep Learning Applications" (arXiv 2408.05148). Search summaries say it shows atomics-driven run-to-run variability on GPUs.
- I could not confirm from Apple primary docs whether the Metal compiler contracts a*b+c into FMA under `safe` mode, or how to disable that (no local `metal` binary to check `-help`). This needs an empirical test: compile a kernel and inspect the AIR or the results.

## Q3. How do differentiable/GPU robotics simulators validate across backends: bitwise or tolerance?

### Takeaway
No GPU robotics simulator I checked demands bitwise equality across backends. MuJoCo's MJX and MuJoCo Warp test against MuJoCo C with element-wise `assert_allclose` at 1e-4 (MJX) to 5e-4 (MJWarp), with comments attributing the gap to float precision. Newton's implicit MPM tests mix bitwise checks (for integer and topology data and sparse matrix structure) with rtol and atol around 1e-6 to 3e-5 for physics quantities, plus invariant checks (yield surface, momentum). Isaac Lab treats PhysX vs Newton as *not numerically portable* and validates by policy transfer. The 2026 GPUSimBench paper documents run-to-run and within-batch variability in Isaac Lab, MuJoCo Warp and others, and compares simulators by distributional metrics (EMD).

### Cited Findings
- **MuJoCo Warp vs MuJoCo C**: `_TOLERANCE = 5e-5` ("tolerance for difference between MuJoCo and mjwarp smooth calculations - mostly due to float precision"), multiplied by 10 "to avoid test noise", giving `assert_allclose(atol=5e-4, rtol=5e-4)` on `qpos`, `qvel`, `qfrc_*`, `act` and others after stepping. Sensor tests use atol 1e-3 to 2e-3. — [mujoco_warp forward_test.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_warp/main/mujoco_warp/_src/forward_test.py), [smooth_test.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_warp/main/mujoco_warp/_src/smooth_test.py)
- **MJX vs MuJoCo C**: `_TOLERANCE = 1e-5` times 10, so atol and rtol are 1e-4, loosened to 5e-4 times 10 for `qvel` in some tests. — [mjx forward_test.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/mjx/mujoco/mjx/_src/forward_test.py)
- **NVIDIA Warp unit tests**: `assert_np_equal(result, expect, tol=0.0)` is the default (exact equality) and switches to `assert_allclose(atol=tol)` when `tol` is non-zero. Per-dtype tolerances in arithmetic tests are about 1e-3 (fp16), 1e-6 (fp32) and 1e-8 (fp64). — [newton unittest_utils.py, same helper as Warp's](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/newton-physics/newton/main/newton/tests/unittest_utils.py); [vendored Warp test_arithmetic.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/qbhf2/GarmentCode/blob/main/NvidiaWarp-GarmentCode/warp/tests/test_arithmetic.py) (third-party copy, may be an older version)
- **Newton implicit MPM tests** (`newton/tests/test_implicit_mpm*.py`): sparse matrix offsets, columns *and values* are compared with `.tobytes()` (bitwise) between two code paths on the same device; elsewhere `rtol=2e-6, atol=2e-6`, `rtol=3e-5` for axial forces vs analytic `E·strain·h²`, and `atol=1e-6` for momentum and force sums. The flow-rule tests check invariants such as the yield bound with tol 1e-3 rather than reference values. Some tests run only on CUDA devices (`get_cuda_test_devices`). — [newton tests tree](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton/tree/main/newton/tests), e.g. [test_implicit_mpm.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/newton-physics/newton/main/newton/tests/test_implicit_mpm.py)
- **Isaac Lab 3.0**: PhysX is "the reference for behavior parity", and Newton (MJWarp, Kamino, VBD, MPM solvers) is Beta. Solver settings and behaviour "are not numerically portable between solver families"; "expect to retune a task when changing solvers". Cross-backend validation is a "Transfer Policies Between PhysX and Newton" workflow. — [Isaac Lab physics backends docs (v3.0.0-EA)](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/v3.0.0-EA/source/concepts/physics_backends.html), [v3.0.0-beta2](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/v3.0.0-beta2/source/overview/core-concepts/physical-backends/index.html)
- NVIDIA reports an ANYmal-D policy trained on Newton deployed in PhysX Isaac Lab as a Sim2Sim demonstration, a single qualitative demo. — [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/?p=106618)
- **Genesis**: seeding does not guarantee bit-for-bit determinism on GPU. `debug=True` enables deterministic algorithms, but it is slow and "only partially supported on GPU backends", and most reliable on `gs.cpu`. Backends include `gs.cuda`, `gs.metal` and `gs.cpu` through the Quadrants compiler. — [Genesis docs: initialization](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/user_guide/configuration/initialization.md.txt)
- **GPUSimBench** (Zhang et al., arXiv 2607.13059, 2026-07-16): a rolling ball hits a 3×3×3 cube array, with 16 envs × 10 runs and fixed seeds.
  - Within-batch and run-to-run spread of final positions (cm):

    | Simulator | Within-batch | Run-to-run |
    |---|---|---|
    | Isaac Lab | 4.21 | 0 |
    | ManiSkill | 4.76 | 0 |
    | Genesis | 0 | 0 |
    | MJX | 0 | 0 |
    | MuJoCo Warp | 4.55 | 2.15 |
    | Madrona | 32.53 | 11.47 |
    | MuJoCo Playground | 0 | 1.39 |

  - Causes given: atomics and parallel-reduction order, which flip contact ordering, active-constraint sets and early termination.
  - Simulators are compared with Earth Mover's Distance to real-world outcome distributions.
  - Limitation: a single hardware stack.

  — [GPUSimBench (HF papers mirror)](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.13059.md)

### Inferences
- The established practice is: bitwise for discrete and integer structure and same-device code-path equivalence; tight tolerance (about 1e-6 to 1e-4 relative) for single-step or few-step continuous quantities; invariants and analytic checks for constitutive models; and distributional or task-level metrics for long contact-rich rollouts. metalsim's parity harness can follow the same tiers: per-kernel bitwise (when FMA, transcendentals and reduction order are pinned), per-step tolerance, and multi-step statistics.
- Because Isaac Lab and MuJoCo Warp are themselves non-deterministic run-to-run or within-batch on CUDA (GPUSimBench), a parity claim against them should be framed as "Metal-vs-CUDA difference is within CUDA-vs-CUDA spread". The CUDA run-to-run spread is the natural noise floor.

### Gaps
- I did not find Brax, Drake or Warp-internal CPU-vs-CUDA cross-device test tolerances specifically. Warp's suite mostly runs each test per device against analytic or NumPy expectations rather than comparing devices to each other (inference from the test-helper design, not verified across the suite).
- I could not open Isaac Lab's "Transfer Policies Between PhysX and Newton" page, and found no quantitative PhysX-vs-Newton parity numbers.
- I found no Genesis issue documenting a quantified Metal-vs-CUDA discrepancy.

## Q4. Chaos and parity: comparing chaotic simulations (granular, DEM, MPM) across implementations

### Takeaway
For chaotic many-body systems the literature agrees that trajectory-level equality is meaningless beyond a few Lyapunov times. Comparisons are done by (a) ensembles and uncertainty quantification (molecular dynamics: Coveney, Wan et al.), (b) shadowing arguments (a numerical orbit stays close to *some* true orbit for tens of Lyapunov times, but not for the specific reference orbit), and (c) for granular codes, benchmark suites comparing macroscopic observables and distributions: discharge rates, velocity histograms, mixing indices, runout. Exact-match tests are kept for single-contact impacts with analytic solutions.

### Cited Findings
- **DEM multi-code benchmark**: Dosta et al., "Comparing open-source DEM frameworks for simulations of common bulk processes", *Computer Physics Communications* 296 (2024) 109066 (published March 2024).
  - Nine open-source codes: Blaze-DEMGPU, ESyS-Particle, GranOO, Kratos, LIGGGHTS, MercuryDPM, MFiX, MUSEN and YADE.
  - Benchmarks: silo emptying, drum mixing and particle impact, restricted to spheres and Hertz-Mindlin contact.
  - Scripts are published as a dataset.
  - Differences are attributed to contact models, wall representation, data structures and parallelization.

  — [Newcastle eprints](https://eprints.ncl.ac.uk/295860), [FAU CRIS](https://cris.fau.de/publications/316355847/). A search snippet of an arXiv comparative-analysis paper describes "eight different DEM codes" compared with particle-velocity histograms per code. — [arXiv 2502.21032](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21032) (snippet only; the code count conflicts with Dosta's nine, so they may be different studies)
- **Particle-impact verification**: Chung & Ooi benchmark tests verify contact-law implementation in DEM codes against analytic solutions, with discrepancies below 0.1%. — [Benchmark tests for verifying DEM codes at particle impact level](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225514307_Benchmark_tests_for_verifying_discrete_element_modelling_codes_at_particle_impact_level)
- **DEM data benchmarking with LAMMPS**: shearing an FCC array of monosized spheres gives a dataset intended for benchmarking new DEM codes against macroscopic behaviour. — [On data benchmarking and verification of discrete granular simulations (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10964069/)
- **MPM comparisons**: column collapse is the standard MPM granular benchmark against experiments and other methods (MPM vs DEM-LBM, Ceccato et al. 2020; MPM variants compared, UCL). I found no multi-*code* MPM round-robin. — [arXiv 2206.07169 (hybrid FEM-MPM column collapse)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07169), [arXiv 2012.13862 (FV-MPM)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.13862), [UCL Discovery 10171972](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10171972)
- **Ensemble methods in chaotic MD**: Wan, Sinclair & Coveney (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A, 2021) argue that because MD is chaotic, ensemble methods ("a sufficiently large number of replicas … from which reliable statistics can be extracted") are needed whatever the run length. Without ensemble averaging, predicted free energy is about 5 times more uncertain (attribution to be checked). — [Uncertainty quantification in classical molecular dynamics (PMC8059622)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8059622/), [CWI repository](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/31202)
- **Shadowing**: Hayes & Jackson found shadows lasting about 50 Lyapunov times for a 180-dimensional Hamiltonian system with local error around 1e-4, although that error reaches O(1) after about 9 Lyapunov times. Shadow duration scales with local error and hyperbolicity, and unstable-dimension variability makes systems unshadowable. In unsoftened n-body systems shadow duration scales as 1/n. Verifying a shadow costs O(N³). — [Hayes, arXiv astro-ph/0209508](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0209508), [Hayes & Jackson fast shadowing (Toronto)](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~wayne/research/thesis/DNA.html)
- **GPU robotics**: GPUSimBench uses outcome distributions and EMD rather than trajectories, because contact-order flips amplify differences at the ULP level. — [GPUSimBench](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.13059.md)

### Inferences
- For MPM sand or granular parity between Metal and CUDA, a defensible protocol modelled on the above is:
  1. Bitwise or near-ULP checks on single kernels and single steps with pinned FMA, transcendentals and reduction order.
  2. Measure the divergence growth rate (effective Lyapunov exponent) of CUDA-vs-CUDA perturbed runs, e.g. a 1-ULP initial perturbation or different atomic orders.
  3. Require that Metal-vs-CUDA divergence grows no faster than, and stays within, the CUDA-vs-CUDA ensemble envelope.
  4. Compare macroscopic observables: runout distance, final pile angle, discharge rate, centre-of-mass trajectory, velocity histograms. Use ensemble statistics (mean and confidence interval, EMD or KS) as DEM benchmarks and GPUSimBench do.
- Many-body granular contact is likely closer to the "unsoftened, glitchy" regime (contact on/off events) than to the smooth hyperbolic one, so expect short shadowing horizons. Trajectory-level parity beyond the first few hundred steps should not be expected unless every operation is pinned bitwise.
